Experiment 1 — Intensity in a two-beam pattern and fringe visibility

Two coherent plane waves meet at a small angle α. Change the beam irradiances, the angle between their polarization directions and the wavelength, and compare the visibility measured from the pattern with theory.

Try this

  1. Set I₂/I₁ = 0.25. Predict V before reading it, then check. How small must I₂/I₁ be for V to fall below 0.5?
  2. With equal beams, increase θ from 0° to 90°. At which θ does V = 0.5? Why are crossed polarizations unable to interfere?
  3. Double α. What happens to Λ? Which angle gives Λ = 10 µm at 633 nm?
  4. Change λ only: does the visibility depend on the wavelength? Explain.

Experiment 2 — Visibility and temporal coherence

A two-beam interferometer with equal beams is lit by a source with a Gaussian spectrum. Every wavelength makes its own fringes and they add in intensity; the fringes fade as the optical path difference D grows. The interferogram is computed by adding 401 spectral components and compared with the analytical visibility |γ(D)|.

l_c = λ₀² / Δλ
V(D) = |γ(D)| = exp[−(πΔσD)² / (4 ln 2)], Δσ = Δλ/λ₀²

Try this

  1. Compare the coherence lengths of the He–Ne laser, the LED and the 100 nm broadband source. Which could you use for holography of a 10 cm deep object?
  2. For the LED (600 nm, 20 nm), find D at which the fringes have visibility 0.5. Check it with the formula 2 ln 2/(πΔσ).
  3. Why are white-light fringes useful for finding the position of zero path difference?
  4. Halve Δλ. By what factor does l_c change?

Experiment 3 — Interference with a point source, and the effect of an extended source

Part A: two coherent point sources (images of one source) are added as exact spherical waves on a screen. Part B: an extended, incoherent source of width w lights two pinholes; the intensities from all source points add.

Try this

  1. A1: double d. Does the measured fringe spacing follow λz/d?
  2. A2: increase s. What happens to the number of rings on the screen, and why are the rings crowded at the edge?
  3. B: find the source width at which the fringes vanish for d = 1 mm. Then move just past it: why do the fringes come back with a bright centre turned dark?
  4. B: halve d. What largest source width now keeps V ≥ 0.5? Relate this to fringe localization.
