Experiment 1 — Spatial-frequency requirements: maximum angle and aliasing

Two waves at angle θ make fringes of frequency f = 2 sin(θ/2)/λ. A camera with pitch Δx records them correctly only if f ≤ f_N = 1/(2Δx); the pixels also reduce the modulation as |sinc(f·ff·Δx)|. Above the limit the carrier folds to |f − k/Δx| and the image appears in the wrong place.

θ_max = 2 arcsin(λ/4Δx) ≈ λ/2Δx
f_seen = |f − k/Δx|, M = |sinc(f·ff·Δx)|

Try this

  1. For each preset camera, find θ_max at 633 nm and at 532 nm. Check λ/(2Δx).
  2. Increase θ above θ_max. Where does the carrier peak move? On which side will the image appear?
  3. At the Nyquist limit, how much modulation is left for ff = 1 and ff = 0.5? Why do engineers keep the carrier near f_N/2?
  4. Why can infrared (1064 nm) and long-wave holography use larger angles with the same camera?

Experiment 2 — Recording set-ups: minimum distance and resolution

Every object point must reach every pixel within θ_max. In-line: the object (width W) is centred; off-axis: it is shifted by about 1.5W so that the image clears the |O|² halo, which needs about four times the distance.

d_min (in-line) = Δx(W + NΔx)/λ
d_min (off-axis) ≈ Δx(4W + NΔx)/λ
Resolution δξ ≈ λd/(NΔx)

λ = 633 nm.

Experiment 3 — Stability: motion during the exposure

The camera integrates the fringes over the exposure. A mirror that drifts by Δ, vibrates with amplitude a, or jitters with rms phase σ washes them out. In reflection the path changes by twice the motion. The fringe visibility is simulated by averaging many instants and compared with theory; only the fringes carry the image, so the image energy falls as V².

Δ = v t_exp, V = |sinc(2Δ/λ)|
V ≥ 0.9 needs t_exp ≤ λ/(8v)
V = |J₀(4πa/λ)|
V = 0.9 at 4πa/λ = 0.641
V = exp(−σ²/2)
V = 0.9 at σ = 0.46 rad
Recorded fringes: still (top) and with motion during the exposure (bottom)

Try this

  1. Drift at 10 µm/s, λ = 532 nm: find the longest exposure for V ≥ 0.9 and check λ/(8v).
  2. Which drift Δ makes the fringes vanish completely? Why does the visibility come back a little beyond it?
  3. Vibration: which amplitude gives V = 0.9? Why is this only a few tens of nanometres?
  4. For what visibility is the image energy only 1 %? Explain why a bright hologram can give an empty reconstruction.
