Experiment 1 — Fourier series, the FFT and convolution

Binary (square-wave) fringes are built from odd harmonics; a sampled carrier line has a discrete spectrum with step 1/L; smoothing by a moving average multiplies the spectrum by H(f) = sinc(fw) — the convolution theorem.

square(x) = ½ + Σ c_n cos(2πnx/p)
c_n = (2/nπ) sin(nπ/2)
N = 1024 samples over L; frequency step 1/L
H(f) = sin(πfw)/(w sin πf) ≈ sinc(fw), f in cycles/sample

Try this

  1. Series: increase n_max from 9 to 99. Does the overshoot at the edges disappear? Read its value (Gibbs ≈ 9 % of the jump).
  2. FFT: set 30.0 and then 30.5 fringes. Why does the peak spread over many bins (leakage) in the second case? What does the Hann window change?
  3. Convolution: choose w so that the first zero of H(f) falls on the carrier. What happens to the fringes?
  4. Check that the FFT ratio of the smoothed and original spectra follows H(f).
الحدس الفيزيائي: كل نمط هدب هو مجموع موجات جيبية، وتحويل فورييه يبيّن مقدار كل منها. والتنعيم بالمتوسط المتحرك ليس إلا ضرباً للطيف في دالة sinc، فيقتل الترددات العالية ومعها الضجيج.

Experiment 3 — Fourier-transform (Takeda) analysis and phase shifting

I = a + b cos(2πf₀x + φ(x, y)) + noise. Takeda: FFT → keep one sideband → shift to the origin → inverse FFT → wrapped phase → unwrap. Four-step phase shifting uses four frames and no carrier.

Takeda: φ_w = arg c(x, y)
4-step: φ = atan2(I₄ − I₂, I₁ − I₃), σ_φ ≈ σ/(b√2)
Central row: true phase (dashed) and recovered phase

Try this

  1. Takeda: shrink the filter radius to 0.03. Why does the phase lose detail? Enlarge it to 0.25 or more: why does the error rise again?
  2. Increase the phase amplitude until the recovered phase breaks. Relate the limit to the carrier frequency (the sideband must not overlap the DC lobe).
  3. Phase shifting: check that the error follows σ/(b√2). Why does phase shifting need no carrier?
  4. Raise the noise to 35 grey levels. Where does simple unwrapping fail first?
