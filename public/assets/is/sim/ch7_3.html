Experiment 1 — Off-axis digital hologram: Fresnel reconstruction, DC term and twin image

A diffusely reflecting object at distance d sends its wave O to a camera (256 × 256 pixels of 6.45 µm). A tilted plane reference R = exp(i2πf_R x) is added; the camera records H = |O + R|². One FFT (the discrete Fresnel transform) reconstructs the image with pixel λd/(NΔx).

Γ = Fresnel₋d{H}, Δξ = λd/(NΔx)
Image offset = λd f_R; twin on the opposite side, defocused
Hologram (centre crop) · its spectrum · Fresnel reconstruction |Γ| (bright values clipped)

Try this

  1. Set f_R = 0. Why do the image, the twin and the DC term pile up in the centre?
  2. Increase f_R step by step. At what carrier is the image free of the DC term? Compare the image offset with λd f_R.
  3. Compare the three DC suppression methods. Which one also removes the twin image?
  4. Double d. How do the image pixel and the field of view change?
الحدس الفيزيائي: تحويل فرينل الواحد يعيد بناء ثلاثة أشياء معاً: الحد الصفري في المركز، والصورة الحقيقية مزاحة بمقدار λd f_R، والتوأم في الجهة المقابلة غير مركَّز. الحامل يفصل بينها، والترشيح يزيل ما لا نحتاجه.

Experiment 2 — Convolution reconstruction, numerical focusing and quantitative phase

A transparent sample (three "cells", phase only) is placed 15 mm in front of the camera (256 × 256 pixels of 3.45 µm). The +1 order is filtered, moved to the centre and propagated back with the angular-spectrum transfer function. The image pixel stays equal to the camera pixel.

U = ASM₋d{ filtered +1 order }
Focus metric: variance of |U| (minimum for a phase object)
Hologram crop · amplitude |U| · phase arg U at d_rec

Try this

  1. Move d_rec away from 15 mm. What happens to the amplitude image of a pure phase object?
  2. Press Autofocus. How close to 15 mm does the minimum of the focus metric fall?
  3. At focus, read the phase error. How does it change with camera noise?
  4. Raise the peak phase above 2π. What new step is needed before the phase can be used?

Experiment 3 — Phase-shifting and lensless Fourier holography

In-line (no carrier): one hologram gives image, twin and DC term on top of each other. Four holograms with reference phases 0, π/2, π, 3π/2 give the complex wave directly: O = [(I₁ − I₃) + i(I₂ − I₄)]/(4R*). Lensless Fourier: a point reference in the object plane cancels the quadratic phase, so one FFT gives the image.

Steps 0, π/2 + ε, π + 2ε, 3π/2 + 3ε
Image and twin are mirror images about the centre, offset ±ξ_r

Try this

  1. Phase shifting: compare the single in-line reconstruction with the four-step one. What has disappeared?
  2. Add a phase-step error of 10°. What appears again in the reconstruction, and how large is it?
  3. Lensless Fourier: set ξ_r = 0. Why do image and twin overlap? What is the smallest offset that separates them?
  4. Why can phase-shifting use the full camera bandwidth for the object while off-axis holography cannot?
