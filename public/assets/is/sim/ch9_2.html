Experiment 1 — Deformation by digital holographic interferometry

Two reconstructed speckle fields of a centrally loaded plate, Γ₁ (before) and Γ₂ (after). The interference phase is Δφ = arg(Γ₂Γ₁*); a sine/cosine filter removes the speckle noise; unwrapping and w = λΔφ/4π give the out-of-plane displacement (normal illumination and observation, λ = 633 nm). An in-plane shift of the speckles between the states decorrelates them.

Δφ = arg(Γ₂Γ₁*), w = λΔφ/4π
Δφ_f = atan2(⟨sin Δφ⟩, ⟨cos Δφ⟩)
Q = intensity-weighted |⟨exp[i(Δφ − Δφ_f)]⟩|
|Γ₁| (speckle) · raw Δφ · filtered Δφ · displacement w
Central profile: true w (dashed) and DHI result

Try this

  1. Compare the error with filter windows 1, 3, 7 and 11. When does a large window start to blur the fringes near the edge?
  2. Increase w₀ until the result breaks. How many fringes per window does that correspond to?
  3. Move the speckle shift from 0 to 1 speckle. Follow Q: at which shift is the phase lost?
  4. Why does each state reconstruct a perfect speckle image even when Q is almost zero?

Experiment 2 — Sensitivity vector, 3-D displacement and two-wavelength contouring

Δφ = (2π/λ) S·d with S = e_o − e_i. Three illuminations give three equations for (u, v, w). Shape: two wavelengths give the synthetic wavelength Λ = λ₁λ₂/|λ₁ − λ₂|; hierarchical unwrapping uses the coarse synthetic height to pick the fringe order of λ₁.

λ = 532 nm, azimuths 0°, 120°, 240°
d = (λ/2π) M⁻¹Δφ
σ_d = (λσ_φ/2π)√diag[(MᵀM)⁻¹]
Λ = λ₁λ₂/|λ₁ − λ₂|, h = ΛΔφ_syn/4π
m = round[(4πh_s/λ₁ − φ₁)/2π]
Safe if 3σ_h < λ₁/4

Try this

  1. 3-D: at α = 30° read the predicted and simulated errors of u, v, w. Why is w measured about five times better?
  2. 3-D: which α minimises the in-plane error? What happens to the out-of-plane error?
  3. Contouring: with 633/612 nm and σ = 0.02 rad, are there wrong orders? Raise σ until they appear and compare with 3σ_h = λ₁/4.
  4. Contouring: bring λ₂ closer to λ₁. Range grows — what happens to reliability?

Experiment 3 — Phase-shifting DHI of a hot-air plume

A slab of air of length L along the beam is heated. Gladstone–Dale: n − 1 = (n₀ − 1)T₀/T, so the phase Δφ = (2π/λ)L(n − n₀) falls as the gas warms. Four phase-shifted frames (8-bit, with noise) give the phase of the cold and hot states; their difference gives the temperature field (λ = 633 nm, T₀ = 293 K, n₀ − 1 = 2.92 × 10⁻⁴).

|dΔφ/dT| = 2πL(n₀ − 1)/(λT₀) at T₀
T = (n₀ − 1)T₀ / (n₀ − 1 + λΔφ/2πL)
Hot-state frame 1 · wrapped Δφ · recovered temperature (°C) · error (K)
Temperature along the central row: true (dashed) and measured

Try this

  1. Read the phase sensitivity and the temperature change per fringe. Check 2πL(n₀ − 1)/(λT₀).
  2. Double L. How do the sensitivity and the temperature error change?
  3. Raise ΔT_max to 150 K and switch to the linear conversion. How large is the error of the linear law at the peak?
  4. Why is the result an average along the beam, and what is needed for a true 3-D temperature field?
