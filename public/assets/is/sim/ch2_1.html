Experiment 1 — The Michelson interferometer

Rings: parallel mirrors and an extended source, fringes of equal inclination seen in the focal plane of a lens (f = 200 mm). Tilt: collimated light with one mirror tilted — straight fringes, bent by a test surface with a curvature error (Twyman–Green).

2d cos θ = mλ (bright rings)
N = 2Δd/λ fringes pass the centre
Spacing λ/(2α); a sag h bends a fringe by 2h/λ fringe spacings

Aperture 20 mm. The sag is given in wavelengths.

Try this

  1. Rings: press ▶ and watch the centre. Do the rings collapse into the centre or come out of it as d decreases? Count the fringes for Δd = 1 µm and compare with 2Δd/λ.
  2. Reduce d towards 0. Why do the rings grow and the field become uniform at d = 0?
  3. Tilt: find the tilt that gives 10 fringes across the 20 mm aperture.
  4. Tilt: set the sag to +0.5λ and then −0.5λ. How can you tell a bump from a dent from the fringe shape?

Experiment 2 — The Mach–Zehnder interferometer

Each beam splitter is a 2 × 2 unitary matrix B = [t r; r t] with r = i√R, t = √T. The output is E_out = B₂ · diag(e^{iφ₁}, e^{iφ₂}) · B₁ · [1; 0]. Below, a phase object (a hot-gas plume) in one arm bends tilted fringes.

50 : 50: I₃ = sin²(Δφ/2), I₄ = cos²(Δφ/2)
R₁ = R₂ = R: V = 2RT/(R² + T²)
Output intensities versus Δφ (from the matrix product) — dots: current Δφ
Output port 4 with a hot-gas plume in one arm and a tilted reference

Try this

  1. With 50 : 50 splitters, check that I₃ + I₄ = 1 for every Δφ. Where does the energy go when port 3 is dark?
  2. Set R = 0.3 for both splitters. Read V and check 2RT/(R² + T²).
  3. Use R₁ = 0.3 and R₂ = 0.7. Why is the visibility 1 again in one port?
  4. Plume: how many fringes does a peak phase of 4π shift a fringe by? Turn the tilt to 0: what pattern do you see now?

Experiment 3 — The Michelson as a Fourier-transform spectrometer

Scanning the optical path difference x records I(x) = ∫S(σ)[1 + cos 2πσx] dσ. The spectrum is the Fourier transform of the AC part. Two lines are resolved only if x_max exceeds about 1/Δσ.

Resolution δσ ≈ 1/x_max
Resolving power σ/δσ = σ x_max
Recovered spectrum |FT of the AC part| (true line positions dashed)

Try this

  1. Sodium doublet: find the smallest x_max that resolves the two lines. Compare with 1/Δσ.
  2. Why does the interferogram of the doublet show beats? Relate the beat period to Δσ.
  3. Switch apodization on. What happens to the side lobes and to the line width?
  4. Close pair (0.1 nm): what scan length is needed? What resolving power is that?
