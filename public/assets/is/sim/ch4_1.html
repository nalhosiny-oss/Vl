Experiment 1 — Photon statistics and the photomultiplier

Photoelectrons from a steady beam are Poisson distributed: mean N, variance N, so SNR = √N. A photomultiplier multiplies each electron at n dynodes (mean gain δ per dynode); the random multiplication adds an excess noise factor F.

Poisson: SNR = √N
G = δⁿ, F = 1 + (1 − δ⁻ⁿ)/(δ − 1) ≈ δ/(δ − 1)
PMT: SNR = √(N/F); photodiode: SNR = N/√(N + σ_amp²)
Histogram of photoelectron counts in 5000 measurements (bars) and the Poisson law (line)
SNR versus mean photoelectrons: PMT (excess noise F) and photodiode + amplifier (dot: current N)

Try this

  1. Set N = 10 and N = 1000. Do the measured SNRs follow √N? Why does the histogram look Gaussian for large N?
  2. Change δ from 3 to 6. How does the excess noise factor F change, and why does the first dynode matter most?
  3. With σ_amp = 100 electrons, below which N does the PMT beat the photodiode? Above which N does it lose?
  4. Why can a PMT count single photons while a photodiode with an amplifier cannot?

Experiment 2 — CCD/CMOS pixel noise and the photon transfer curve

Pixel model: signal = Poisson(μ + dark) + read noise, clipped at the full well; output DN = offset + K·signal. Two flat frames are subtracted to remove fixed-pattern noise; the variance–mean slope gives the conversion gain K and the intercept gives the read noise.

var(DN) = K·μ_DN + K²σ_r²
SNR = μ/√(μ + σ_r²), DR = FW/σ_r
Photon transfer curve: variance versus mean (from frame pairs) and the fitted line
A recorded fringe pattern at the chosen exposure (row profile)

Try this

  1. Read K and σ_r from the fit and compare them with the sliders. Why must two frames be subtracted?
  2. Lower the exposure to ~30 electrons. Which noise dominates now? At which signal do read noise and shot noise become equal?
  3. Raise the exposure above the full well. What happens to the fringe peaks and to the photon transfer curve?
  4. Compute the dynamic range in dB for FW = 20 000 e⁻ and σ_r = 10 e⁻.

Experiment 3 — Sampling fringes with a pixel array: pixel MTF and aliasing

Fringes I(x) = 1 + cos(2πx/p) are integrated over each pixel of width a = ff·Δ and sampled every Δ. The recorded modulation is |sinc(a/p)|; fringes finer than two pixels per period alias.

M = |sinc(ff·Δ/p)|, Nyquist p ≥ 2Δ
f_seen = |f − k/Δ|; after n transfers Q = CTEⁿ
True fringes (thin) and the pixel values (steps) over 24 pixels
Pixel MTF: recorded modulation versus fringe period (dot: current setting)

Try this

  1. Set p = 2 (Nyquist). What modulation is recorded for ff = 1 and ff = 0.5?
  2. Set p = 1.25. How many pixels per period do the samples seem to show? Check with f_seen = |f − 1/Δ|.
  3. Why does a smaller fill factor raise the MTF but also let aliased frequencies through?
  4. CTE: what fraction of the charge from the far corner of a 2048 × 2048 array survives for CTE = 0.99999?
