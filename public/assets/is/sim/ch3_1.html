Experiment 1 — From a sum of beams to the Airy function

A plane-parallel plate with surface reflectance R transmits the beams T(Re^{iδ})^k, k = 0, 1, 2, … Add N of them and watch the sum approach the Airy function. A small absorption A per surface (T = 1 − R − A) lowers the peaks.

I_t/I₀ = [T/(1 − R)]² / [1 + F sin²(δ/2)]
F = 4R/(1 − R)², N_F = π√R/(1 − R)

N = 201 is shown as the full Airy sum.

Partial sum of N beams (solid) and the Airy function (dashed)
Phasor sum of the transmitted beams at the current δ (drag the δ slider)

Try this

  1. For R = 0.9, how many beams are needed before the peak reaches 95 % of the Airy value? Relate this to 1/(1 − R).
  2. Compare the measured finesse with π√R/(1 − R) for R = 0.5, 0.9 and 0.97.
  3. Switch to reflection: check that I_t + I_r = 1 when A = 0. What happens to this sum when A = 0.02?
  4. Watch the phasor diagram at δ = 0 and at δ slightly away from 0: why do the beams almost cancel off resonance?

Experiment 2 — The Fabry–Perot interferometer as a spectrometer

Two close spectral lines pass through an etalon. Rings form in the focal plane of a lens (f = 300 mm): 2nd cos θ = mλ. A scanning Fabry–Perot records the centre spot while the plate separation changes.

FSR_λ = λ²/(2nd), δλ = FSR/N_F
Resolving power λ/δλ = mN_F

λ₁ = 632.8 nm (line 1); line 2 = λ₁ + Δλ, equal strength. Δλ is in picometres.

Rings in the focal plane (24 mm × 24 mm)
Scanning Fabry–Perot: centre-spot signal versus change of separation (1.3 free spectral ranges)

Try this

  1. With d = 5 mm and R = 0.95, find the smallest Δλ at which two separate peaks appear. Compare with FSR/N_F (Taylor) and 0.58 FSR/N_F (Sparrow).
  2. Increase Δλ beyond the FSR (≈ 40 pm). Why does the second line appear on the "wrong" side?
  3. Double d. What happens to the FSR and to the resolving power?
  4. Which etalon would you choose to study a line structure spread over 100 pm with 1 pm detail?

Experiment 3 — Fringes of equal thickness and FECO

A: a silvered air wedge with a small step of height h, seen in reflection at λ = 546.1 nm. B: fringes of equal chromatic order — white light through a silvered film is dispersed by a spectrograph; bright fringes satisfy 2t = mλ.

Adjacent fringes: Δt = λ/2
A step h shifts the fringes by 2h/λ of an order
2t = mλ_m; a step h moves order m by Δλ = 2h/m

Try this

  1. A: compare R = 0.04 and R = 0.9 for a 20 nm step. With which fringes can you see the step, and why?
  2. A: what step height shifts the fringes by exactly half an order?
  3. B: read the order m of the fringe near 546 nm and the shift Δλ. Recover h = mΔλ/2 and compare with the slider.
  4. B: why is FECO more sensitive than equal-thickness fringes for very small steps on thin films?
