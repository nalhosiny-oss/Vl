Experiment 1 — Absorption, quantum efficiency and the p-i-n bandwidth trade-off

A thicker absorber collects more light but carriers take longer to cross it; a thinner one has more capacitance. The best width balances the transit-time and RC limits.

InGaAs: Eg = 0.75 eV, α(1.55 µm) = 0.8 µm⁻¹, vsat = 5×10⁴ m/s, εr = 13.9
η = 1 − e−αW, R = ηλ[µm]/1.24
ftr = 0.45vsat/W, fRC = 1/(2πRLC), C = εA/W
Wopt = √(0.45vsat·2πRLεA)

Try this

  1. For W = 1, 2 and 3 µm, read η and R at 1.55 µm. Why does R saturate?
  2. Find the cut-off wavelength. Why does InGaAs not respond at 1.7 µm?
  3. Compare the numerical optimum W with Wopt. What is the maximum bandwidth for D = 20 µm?
  4. Halve the diameter. How do Wopt and the maximum bandwidth change? Why do fast photodiodes have tiny areas?

Experiment 2 — Receiver noise, SNR and the optimum avalanche gain

A p-i-n receiver is thermal-limited at low power (SNR ∝ P²) and shot-limited at high power (SNR ∝ P). Avalanche gain helps until the multiplied shot noise, enlarged by the excess factor F(M), takes over.

R = 0.9 A/W, Id = 10 nA, B = 10 GHz, Rf = 1 kΩ, Fn = 2
SNR = (MRP)²/[2q(RP + Id)M²F(M)B + 4kBTBFn/Rf]
F(M) = kM + (1 − k)(2 − 1/M)
Mopt ≈ [4kBTFn/(kqRf(RP + Id))]1/3

Try this

  1. At P = 10 µW, is the p-i-n receiver thermal- or shot-limited? Where do the two asymptotes cross?
  2. At P = 1 µW, compare the optimum gain for k = 0.02 (Si), 0.1 and 0.5 (InGaAs/InP). Why does a small k help?
  3. Check the numerical Mopt against the cube-root formula.
  4. At P = 1 mW, does the APD still improve the SNR? Explain.

Experiment 3 — Bit-error ratio, Q factor and receiver sensitivity

For on-off keying with Gaussian noise, BER = ½ erfc(Q/√2) with Q = (I₁ − I₀)/(σ₁ + σ₀). The sensitivity is the average power that gives Q = 6 (BER = 10⁻⁹).

BER = ½ erfc(Q/√2)
σT² = 4kBTBFn/Rf, B = Rb/2
σ₁² = 2qM²F(M)(2RP̄)B + σT²
