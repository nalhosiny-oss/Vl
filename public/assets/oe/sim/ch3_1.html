Experiment 1 — Electro-optic amplitude modulator (Pockels cell)

A longitudinal KDP cell between crossed polarisers is a voltage-controlled wave plate: Γ(V) = Γ₀ + πV/Vπ, T = sin²(Γ/2). The transmission is computed both from the sin² law and from the full Jones-matrix product.

λ = 633 nm, no = 1.507
Vπ = λ/(2no³r63) (independent of size)
T = sin²[(Γ₀ + πV/Vπ)/2]
Transmission versus voltage (lines: sin² law; dots: Jones matrices)
Output intensity for V = Vm sin ωt

Try this

  1. Compare Vπ of KDP and KD*P. Why does deuteration lower the half-wave voltage?
  2. Set Γ₀ = 0 and a small drive. Why is the output at twice the drive frequency and very weak?
  3. Set Γ₀ = π/2 (quarter-wave bias). Explain why the response becomes linear.
  4. Increase Vm to 0.4 Vπ with quarter-wave bias. How large is the distortion of the output waveform?

Experiment 2 — Liquid-crystal devices: ECB retarder and twisted-nematic cell

Above the Fréedericksz threshold the directors tilt toward the field and the retardation falls. In a 90° twisted-nematic (TN) cell between parallel polarisers the light follows the twist (waveguiding) when u = 2dΔn/λ is large.

no = 1.50, K11 = 10 pN, Δε = 10, λ = 633 nm
Vth = π√(K11/ε₀Δε)
Γ = 2πd(neff − no)/λ, T = sin²(Γ/2) at 45°
TN: T = sin²[(π/2)√(1 + u²)]/(1 + u²)
ECB cell: retardation Γ/π and transmission versus voltage (dot: current V)
TN cell between parallel polarisers: Gooch–Tarry (line) and 200-slab Jones model (dots)

Try this

  1. Read Vth. Why does nothing happen below it?
  2. Find the voltage at which the ECB cell acts as a half-wave plate (Γ = π). Why are LC devices slow compared with Pockels cells?
  3. For the TN cell, find the first Gooch–Tarry minimum u = √3. What thickness gives it for Δn = 0.2?
  4. Why does a TN display between crossed polarisers look bright with no voltage and dark with voltage?

Experiment 3 — Acousto-optic diffraction: Raman–Nath and Bragg regimes

The Klein–Cook coupled-wave equations are integrated for 17 diffraction orders. Q → 0 gives Raman–Nath diffraction (Im = Jm²(ν)); Q ≫ 1 at the Bragg angle gives a single order with I₁ = sin²(ν/2).

dEm/dξ = (ν/2)[Em−1e−i(Q/2)ξ(α+2m−1) − Em+1e+i(Q/2)ξ(α+2m+1)]
Q = 2πλL/(nΛ²); TeO₂ at 110 MHz, L = 10 mm: Q ≈ 27
Intensity of each diffraction order at the current settings

Try this

  1. Set Q = 0. Check that I₀ = J₀²(ν) and I₁ = J₁²(ν). How many orders appear for ν = 5?
  2. Set Q = 40 and α = −1. At which ν does all the light go into order +1? Compare with sin²(ν/2).
  3. Keep Q = 40 and move α away from −1. How fast does the efficiency drop (angular selectivity)?
  4. Use Q = 5. Why does the device behave in neither limit cleanly?

Experiment 4 — Magneto-optic Faraday rotator and optical isolator

The Faraday rotation θF = VBBL has the same sense for forward and backward light. Polariser (0°) → rotator (45°) → polariser (45°) passes forward light and blocks reflections; any rotation error δ leaks Tback = sin²δ.

TGG at 1064 nm: VB(300 K) = −40 rad T⁻¹ m⁻¹, VB ∝ 1/T
Forward: Tf = cos²(θF − 45°)
Backward: Tb = cos²(θF + 45°) = sin²δ

Try this

  1. Find the rod length that gives exactly 45° at B = 1 T and 300 K.
  2. Heat the rod to 330 K. How much does the rotation drift, and what isolation is left?
  3. What rotation error is allowed for 30 dB isolation? For 40 dB?
  4. Why would an optically active (reciprocal) rotator of 45° not work as an isolator?
