Experiment 1 — Index ellipsoid, birefringence and the Pockels effect in LiNbO₃

A uniaxial crystal has index no for ordinary light and ne(θ) for extraordinary light. An applied field Ez deforms the index ellipsoid through r33, which is the basis of every LiNbO₃ modulator.

no = 2.211, ne = 2.138, r33 = 30.8 pm/V (1.55 µm)
1/n²(θ) = cos²θ/no² + sin²θ/ne²
exact: n′ = (1/n² + rE)−1/2; linear: Δn = −½n³rE
Vπ = λd/(ne³r33LΓ)
Extraordinary index ne(θ) (dot: current θ)
Half-wave voltage versus electrode length (dot: current L)

Try this

  1. Find the thickness of a LiNbO₃ half-wave plate at 1.55 µm from ne − no.
  2. Raise Ez to 30 V/µm. How large is the index change, and how well does the linear formula agree with the exact one?
  3. Read VπL for d = 10 µm and Γ = 0.5. Why is the product VπL, not Vπ, the figure of merit?
  4. Thin-film LiNbO₃ uses d ≈ 5 µm and Γ ≈ 0.8. Estimate the new VπL and compare with bulk devices.

Experiment 2 — Mach–Zehnder modulator: transfer function, harmonics and extinction

Pout/Pin = ½[1 + cos(φb + πV/Vπ)]. A sinusoidal drive V = V₀ sin ωt produces harmonics whose amplitudes are Bessel functions of m = πV₀/Vπ; at quadrature the even harmonics vanish.

H1 = J1(m)|sin φb|, H2 = J2(m)|cos φb|, H3 = J3(m)|sin φb|
ER = [(√a + √b)/(√a − √b)]²
Transfer curve with the drive swing (shaded) and the output waveform
Harmonic amplitudes from a numerical FFT (bars) and Bessel theory (dots)

Try this

  1. Bias at quadrature (φb = −π/2). Check that H2 ≈ 0 and that H1 = J1(m), H3 = J3(m).
  2. Move the bias to φb = 0 (peak) or −π (null). Which harmonic dominates now, and why is this useful for frequency doubling?
  3. For analog links the third harmonic must be 40 dB below the fundamental. Which m satisfies this?
  4. A 52:48 splitter (a = 0.52): what extinction ratio is left? Why do commercial modulators include a tuning section?

Experiment 3 — Advanced modulation formats with a nested (IQ) modulator

Each child MZM is biased at null so its field is E = sin(πV/2Vπ); the parent MZM adds 90° between I and Q. The bit-error ratio is found by Monte Carlo with additive Gaussian noise.

E = sin(πVI/2Vπ) + i sin(πVQ/2Vπ)
QPSK: BER = ½ erfc√(Eb/N₀)
16-QAM (Gray): BER ≈ (3/8) erfc√(0.4 Eb/N₀)
Received constellation (8000 symbols)
BER versus Eb/N₀: theory (lines) and Monte Carlo at the current settings (dot)

Try this

  1. QPSK at Eb/N₀ = 8 dB: compare the Monte Carlo BER with theory. How many errors were counted?
  2. For 16-QAM, raise the drive from 0.3 Vπ to 1.0 Vπ. Why do the outer points crowd together, and what happens to EVM?
  3. How many dB more Eb/N₀ does 16-QAM need than QPSK for BER = 10⁻³? What do you gain in return?
  4. Why is driving QPSK hard (Vpeak = Vπ) harmless, while 16-QAM needs linear drive?
