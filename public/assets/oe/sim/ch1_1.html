Experiment 1 — Coupling coefficient: exact supermodes vs coupled-mode theory

Two identical TE slab guides (core n₁, width w) separated by a gap s in cladding n₂. The exact even and odd supermodes give κ = (βeven − βodd)/2; coupled-mode theory gives a closed form built from the single-guide mode.

Cladding n₂ = 1.45
κSM = (βe − βo)/2
κCMT = 2h²p e−ps / [β₀(w + 2/p)(h² + p²)]
Lc = π/(2κ)
Coupling coefficient versus gap (log scale): exact supermodes and CMT
Even and odd supermode fields at the chosen gap (cores shaded)

Try this

  1. Increase the gap from 0.5 µm to 3 µm. Why does κ fall exponentially? Read the decay constant p from the slope.
  2. At which gap does CMT differ from the exact result by more than 10 %? Explain why CMT fails for strongly coupled guides.
  3. Raise n₁ to 1.58. The mode is more confined: what happens to κ and Lc? Check whether the guide is still single-mode (V < π).
  4. Change λ from 1.3 to 1.6 µm at fixed geometry. Why is a directional coupler wavelength dependent?

Experiment 2 — Power exchange in a directional coupler

The coupled-mode equations are integrated numerically (RK4) and compared with the analytic solution P₂ = (κ²/ψ²) sin²ψz, ψ² = κ² + δ², δ = (β₁ − β₂)/2.

da₁/dz = −iκ a₂ e+2iδz
da₂/dz = −iκ a₁ e−2iδz
F = κ²/(κ² + δ²)
Power in each guide (lines: analytic; dots: RK4)

Try this

  1. With δ = 0 read the coupling length and the 3-dB length. Compare with π/(2κ).
  2. Set δ/κ = 1. What fraction of the power can reach guide 2? Is it consistent with F = κ²/(κ² + δ²)?
  3. Check the card "max |P₁ + P₂ − 1|". Why must the sum stay equal to 1?
  4. Find δ/κ that gives a period of exactly one Lc for P₂. (Hint: ψ = 2κ.)

Experiment 3 — Grating, prism and butt couplers

A grating supplies the missing momentum 2π/Λ; a prism matches β through its high index; a butt joint relies on the overlap of the fibre and waveguide modes.

Grating (λ = 1.55 µm): Λ = λ/(Neff − nc sin θ)
Prism (np = 1.8): Neff = np sin θp
η = [2w₁w₂/(w₁² + w₂²)]² exp[−2d²/(w₁² + w₂²)]

Try this

  1. Find Λ for θ = 8° and θ = 10°. Why are grating couplers usually designed slightly off vertical (θ ≈ 8–10°)?
  2. For a fixed period, how many degrees does the coupling angle move per nm of wavelength? What does this mean for bandwidth?
  3. Set Neff above 1.8: why can the prism no longer couple light into the guide?
  4. With w₁ = 5.2 µm (SMF) and w₂ = 0.5 µm, what is η? Explain why silicon chips need spot-size converters or grating couplers.
