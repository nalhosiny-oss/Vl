Experiment 1 — LED emission spectrum and efficiency

Spontaneous emission follows the joint density of states times the Boltzmann factor. The internal efficiency follows the ABC model, and only light inside the escape cone leaves a planar surface.

I(hν) ∝ (hν − Eg)1/2 e−(hν−Eg)/kT; peak Eg + kT/2, FWHM = 1.795kT
GaAs: Eg(T) = 1.519 − 5.405×10⁻⁴T²/(T + 204) eV
ηint = Bn²/(An + Bn² + Cn³), A = 10⁷ s⁻¹, B = 10⁻¹⁰ cm³/s, C = 10⁻³⁰ cm⁶/s
ηextr = ½(1 − cos θc)[1 − ((nr−1)/(nr+1))²]
Emission spectrum at the current temperature (dashed: 300 K)
Internal quantum efficiency versus carrier density (dot: current n)

Try this

  1. Raise T from 250 to 350 K. How do the peak wavelength and the spectral width change? Check FWHM = 1.795kT.
  2. Find the carrier density of maximum ηint and compare with √(A/C). Why does the efficiency drop at high density (droop)?
  3. For GaAs (nr = 3.6), what fraction of the light escapes a planar surface? What happens for nr = 2.5 (GaN)?
  4. Estimate the external efficiency ηintηextr at n = 10¹⁸ cm⁻³.

Experiment 2 — Laser rate equations and the L–I characteristic

The steady-state rate equations are solved at each current. Above threshold the carrier density clamps at nth and every extra electron becomes a stimulated photon.

dn/dt = I/qV − n/τn − vga(n − ntr)S
dS/dt = Γvga(n − ntr)S − S/τp + Γβn/τn
Γ = 0.3, a = 2.5×10⁻¹⁶ cm², ntr = 10¹⁸ cm⁻³, τn = 2 ns, w = 2 µm, d = 0.2 µm, hν = 0.8 eV
dP/dI = (hν/q)αm/(αi + αm)

Try this

  1. Read αm, τp, nth and Ith for the default laser. Check the slope against (hν/q)αm/(αi + αm).

  3. Increase β to 10⁻². Why does the threshold kink become soft?
  4. Double the cavity length. Why does the threshold current rise even though the threshold gain falls?

Experiment 3 — Laser dynamics: turn-on, relaxation oscillations and modulation response

The rate equations are integrated in time (RK4, 0.05-ps step) for a current step from zero. The small-signal theory predicts the relaxation frequency and the modulation response H(f).

ωR² = vgaS₀/τp, γ = 1/τn + vgaS₀
H(ω) = ωR²/(ωR² − ω² + iωγ)
(same laser as Experiment 2 with default values)

Try this

Experiment 4 — Vacuum deposition: kinetic theory and thickness uniformity (CLO 1.2)

Physical vapour deposition needs a mean free path longer than the chamber and a background that deposits much less than a monolayer during growth. A small evaporation source follows the cosine law.

λmfp = kBT/(√2 πd²p), N₂: d = 0.37 nm, T = 300 K
Φ = p/√(2πmkBT), τML = Ns/Φ, Ns = 10¹⁵ cm⁻²
t(r)/t₀ = [1 + (r/h)²]⁻²
