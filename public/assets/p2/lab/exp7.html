Experiment 7: Self-Inductance of a Coil (RL circuit)

Determining L from the impedance Z of an inductive coil at 60 Hz

⚠️ Safety: read the maximum current printed on the coil and never exceed it; use the low-voltage AC supply only and raise it gradually; switch off before changing connections — breaking the current of a large coil suddenly can give a spark.

Pre-lab questions

Answer before you come to the lab. The correction appears as soon as you choose.

Theory

A real coil has an inductance L and also the ordinary (ohmic) resistance R of its copper wire, so it behaves like R and L in series. Linked to Chapter 6 – inductors in an AC circuit and to self-induction in Chapter 5.

1. Inductive reactance

An inductor opposes changes of current (Lenz’s law). Its opposition to AC is the inductive reactance:

XL = ωL = 2πf L,   f = 60 Hz in Saudi Arabia

2. Impedance of the coil

In the coil the voltage across R is in phase with the current, while the voltage across L leads it by 90°. They add like perpendicular vectors (phasors):

Z = √(R² + XL²)  and  Vrms = Irms Z

3. Method

Measure the current I through the coil for several voltages V across it. Plot V (y-axis) against I (x-axis): a straight line through the origin whose slope is Z (Ohm’s law for AC). Then

XL = √(Z² − R²)  →  L = XL / (2πf)

Here R is the ohmic resistance of the coil itself (written on the coil, or measured with an ohmmeter / with a DC supply). Compare your L with the value written on the coil:

error % = |Lexp − Lcoil| / Lcoil × 100 %
Apparatus: variable low-voltage AC supply (60 Hz), inductive coil (with L, R and maximum current written on it), rheostat (to limit the current), AC ammeter, AC voltmeter, connecting wires.
