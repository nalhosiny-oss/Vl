Experiment 2: Charging and Discharging a Capacitor

Measuring the time constant τ = RC from the charging curve and from a straight-line graph

⚠️ Safety: observe the polarity of an electrolytic capacitor (+ to +) and never exceed its rated voltage; after the experiment, discharge the capacitor through the resistor before touching its leads — a charged capacitor can give a shock or a spark.

Pre-lab questions

Answer before you come to the lab. The correction appears as soon as you choose.

Theory

A capacitor stores charge. When it is connected to a battery through a resistor it does not charge instantly: the current is limited by R, so the voltage grows exponentially. Linked to Chapter 1 – electric field (the field between the plates) and to the RC circuits of Chapter 6.

1. Charging (switch to position A)

VC(t) = ε (1 − e−t/τ),   τ = RC

After one time constant (t = τ) the capacitor has reached 63 % of ε (because 1 − e−1 = 0.632). After 5τ it is practically full (99.3 %).

2. Discharging (switch to position B)

VC(t) = V0 e−t/τ  →  at t = τ, VC = 37 % of V0

3. A straight-line graph

Taking the natural logarithm of the discharge equation:

ln(V0/V) = t / τ  →  slope = 1/τ  →  τ = 1/slope

Plot ln(V0/V) (no unit) on the y-axis against t (s) on the x-axis. Using all the points gives a better τ than reading a single 37 % point.

4. Units

Ω × F = (V/A) × (C/V) = C/A = s. Example: 100 kΩ × 470 µF = 105 × 4.7 × 10−4 = 47 s.

Apparatus: DC power supply, electrolytic capacitor, resistor, two-way switch, digital voltmeter (high resistance), stopwatch, connecting wires.
