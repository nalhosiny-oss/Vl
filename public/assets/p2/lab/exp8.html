Experiment 8: Capacitance of a Capacitor (RC circuit)

Determining C from the impedance Z of a series RC circuit at 60 Hz

⚠️ Safety: use a non-polarised capacitor for AC (an electrolytic capacitor can burst on AC); never exceed the maximum voltage written on the capacitor; switch off and discharge the capacitor before touching its leads.

Pre-lab questions

Answer before you come to the lab. The correction appears as soon as you choose.

Theory

A capacitor connected to AC charges and discharges every half-cycle, so an alternating current flows in the circuit even though no charge crosses the gap between the plates. Linked to Chapter 6 – capacitors in an AC circuit.

1. Capacitive reactance

XC = 1/(ωC) = 1/(2πf C),   f = 60 Hz in Saudi Arabia

XC decreases when the frequency or the capacitance increases. For DC (f = 0) it is infinite: a capacitor blocks steady current.

2. Impedance of the series RC circuit

The voltage across R is in phase with the current, while the voltage across C lags it by 90°. They add as perpendicular vectors:

Z = √(R² + XC²)  and  Vrms = Irms Z

3. Method

Measure the current I for several voltages V across the series combination of R and C. Plot V (y-axis) against I (x-axis): the slope is Z. Then

XC = √(Z² − R²)  →  C = 1 / (2πf XC)

Compare with the value written on the capacitor: error % = |Cexp − Ccap| / Ccap × 100 %.

Apparatus: variable low-voltage AC supply (60 Hz), non-polarised capacitor, fixed resistor R (known), AC ammeter, AC voltmeter, connecting wires.
