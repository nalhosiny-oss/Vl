Experiment 1: Kirchhoff's Laws

Verifying the junction (current) rule and the loop (voltage) rule in a DC circuit

⚠️ Safety: switch the DC supply off while you change connections, start from 0 V and raise the voltage slowly, connect the ammeter in series only (never across a component), and do not exceed the power rating of the resistors.

Theory

Kirchhoff's two rules let us analyse any DC circuit. They are linked to Physics 1 – current and resistance (Ohm's law) and are the basis of all the AC-circuit experiments later in this course.

1. The junction rule (current law, KCL)

At any junction (node), the sum of the currents entering equals the sum of the currents leaving. This is conservation of charge: charge cannot pile up at a point.

Σ Iin = Σ Iout  →  at node A: I1 = I2 + I3

2. The loop rule (voltage law, KVL)

Around any closed loop, the algebraic sum of the potential differences is zero. This is conservation of energy: a charge returning to its starting point has the same potential energy.

Σ ΔV = 0  →  loop 1: ε − V1 − V2 = 0 ;   loop 2: V2 − V3 = 0

3. Our circuit

The supply ε drives current I1 through R1; the current then divides between R2 and R3, which are connected in parallel between nodes A and B. The theoretical values are:

R23 = R2R3 / (R2 + R3),   I1 = ε / (R1 + R23),   V2 = V3 = I1R23 I2 = V2 / R2,   I3 = V3 / R3,   percentage error = |experimental − theoretical| / theoretical × 100 %

4. Using the multimeter

• Voltmeter: connected in parallel (across) the component; it has a very large resistance. • Ammeter: connected in series (the circuit is broken and the meter inserted); it has a very small resistance. • Start on the highest range, then move down to get more significant figures.

Apparatus: DC power supply (0–12 V), three resistors (different values), connection board, connecting wires, digital multimeter (AVO-meter).
