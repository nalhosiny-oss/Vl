Experiment 3: Using the Oscilloscope

Measuring the peak-to-peak voltage, the period and the frequency of AC signals

⚠️ Safety: connect the signal generator to the oscilloscope with the BNC lead (earth clip to earth); never connect the oscilloscope directly to the mains socket; keep the trace brightness moderate and do not leave a bright stationary spot on the screen.

Pre-lab questions

Answer before you come to the lab. The correction appears as soon as you choose.

Theory

The oscilloscope draws a graph of voltage (vertical axis) against time (horizontal axis). It is the main measuring instrument for the alternating-current experiments of Chapter 6 – AC circuits.

1. The two scale controls

• VOLTS/DIV (vertical sensitivity): how many volts one large square represents. • TIME/DIV (sweep speed): how much time one large square represents. The screen has 8 vertical and 10 horizontal divisions; each division has 5 small ticks (0.2 div).

2. Measuring a voltage

Vpp = (number of vertical divisions from peak to peak) × (VOLTS/DIV),   Vm = Vpp/2

3. Measuring the period and the frequency

T = (horizontal divisions for n cycles) × (TIME/DIV) / n,   f = 1/T

Measuring several cycles (n = 2 or 3) and dividing makes the reading error smaller.

4. Uncertainty of a reading

You can read the screen to about ±0.1 div. Example: Vpp = (5.2 ± 0.1 div)(2 V/div) = 10.4 V ± 1.9 % = 10.4 ± 0.2 V. The same percentage carries to f = 1/T.

5. Good practice

Choose VOLTS/DIV so the trace fills most of the screen height, and TIME/DIV so that 2–4 cycles are visible. Make sure the variable (CAL) knobs are in the calibrated position. Use the vertical POSITION knob to put a peak or a trough on a grid line.

Apparatus: oscilloscope, signal (function) generator, frequency counter, AC voltmeter, BNC leads.
