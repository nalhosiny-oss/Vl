Experiment 9: Resonance in a Series RLC Circuit

Finding the resonance frequency f0 from the current–frequency curve and comparing it with 1/(2π√LC)

⚠️ Safety: keep the generator output low (a few volts); at resonance the voltages across L and across C separately can be several times larger than the supply voltage — do not touch the component leads while the circuit is on.

Pre-lab questions

Answer before you come to the lab. The correction appears as soon as you choose.

Theory

In a series RLC circuit the inductor and the capacitor oppose each other. Linked to Chapter 6 – the RLC series circuit and resonance.

1. Impedance

Z = √(RT² + (XL − XC)²),   XL = 2πfL,   XC = 1/(2πfC),   Irms = Vrms/Z

RT is the total resistance of the circuit (the resistor, the coil wire and the generator).

2. Resonance

At low frequency XC is large; at high frequency XL is large. At one frequency they are equal and cancel, Z = RT is minimum and the current is maximum:

XL = XC  →  f0 = 1 / (2π√(LC)),   Imax = Vrms/RT

At resonance the current is in phase with the supply voltage and the power factor cos φ = 1.

3. Method

Keep the generator voltage constant, change the frequency in steps and record the current (or VR, which is proportional to it). Take more readings near the peak. Plot I (y-axis) against f (x-axis); the frequency at the top of the curve is the experimental f0.

4. Sharpness (extra)

The two frequencies where I = Imax/√2 = 0.707 Imax define the bandwidth Δf. The quality factor is Q = f0/Δf: a smaller R gives a sharper (higher-Q) peak. This is how a radio selects one station.

Apparatus: signal generator (sine wave), resistor, inductor (coil), capacitor, AC milliammeter (or a voltmeter across R), AC voltmeter, connecting wires.
