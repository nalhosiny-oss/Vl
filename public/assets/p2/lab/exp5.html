Experiment 5: The Tangent Galvanometer

Measuring the horizontal component BH of the Earth's magnetic field

⚠️ Safety: keep steel objects, magnets, mobile phones and current-carrying wires away from the compass; do not leave a large current flowing in the coil for long (it heats); use the rheostat to start from a small current.

Pre-lab questions

Answer before you come to the lab. The correction appears as soon as you choose.

Theory

A tangent galvanometer is a vertical circular coil with a small compass needle at its centre. Linked to Chapter 4 – sources of the magnetic field (Biot–Savart law: field at the centre of a circular loop).

1. Field of the coil

For n turns of radius r carrying current I, the Biot–Savart law gives the field at the centre, perpendicular to the plane of the coil:

B = μ0 n I / (2r),   μ0 = 4π × 10−7 T·m/A (permeability of free space)

2. Setting up

With no current, the needle points along the Earth's horizontal field BH (magnetic north–south). The coil is turned until its plane contains this direction (the plane of the coil lies in the magnetic meridian). Then the coil's field B is perpendicular to BH.

3. The tangent law

The needle settles along the resultant of the two perpendicular fields, turning through an angle θ where

tan θ = B / BH  →  μ0nI/(2r) = BH tan θ  →  I = K tan θ K = 2r BH / (μ0n)  (reduction factor)  →  BH = μ0 n K / (2r)

4. The graph

Plot I (A) on the y-axis against tan θ on the x-axis: a straight line through the origin whose slope is K.

5. Four readings for each current

Read both ends of the pointer (θ1, θ2), then reverse the current with the reversing key and read again (θ3, θ4). Their average cancels small errors in centring and in aligning the coil. Best accuracy is near θ = 45°; keep θ between about 25° and 65°.

Apparatus: tangent galvanometer (coil with taps for different numbers of turns, compass with pointer and degree scale), DC power supply, rheostat, ammeter, reversing key, connecting wires.
