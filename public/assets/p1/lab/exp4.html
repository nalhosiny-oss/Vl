Theory

Linked to Chapter 8 — Current and Resistance of the lectures.

1. Resistance and Ohm’s law

When a potential difference V is applied across a conductor, a current I flows. The resistance is defined as

R = V / I unit: 1 Ω = 1 V/A

For many materials (metals at constant temperature, carbon resistors) R stays constant over a wide range of voltages. Such a conductor is ohmic and obeys Ohm’s law:

V = I R (R constant)

Ohm’s law is not a fundamental law of nature; it describes some materials only. A lamp filament, a diode and other semiconductor devices are non-ohmic.

2. The straight-line graph

Plot V (V) on the y-axis against I (A) on the x-axis. For an ohmic resistor the points lie on a straight line through the origin and

R = slope = ΔV / ΔI

Convert the ammeter reading from mA to A first: 25.0 mA = 25.0 × 10⁻³ A = 0.0250 A.

3. The circuit

The supply, switch S, rheostat Rh, ammeter A and the resistor R are in series; the voltmeter V is connected in parallel across R. Changing the rheostat changes the current, and with it the voltage across R.
