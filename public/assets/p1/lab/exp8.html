Theory

Linked to Chapter 9 — Light and Optics (refraction, the nature of light) of the lectures.

1. Refraction

Light travels in straight lines in one medium. At the boundary between two media part of it enters the second medium and changes direction, because light has a different speed in each medium. The refractive index of a medium is

n = c / v (c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum)

n = 1 for vacuum and n > 1 for transparent materials such as glass and water in visible light; for air n = 1.0003 ≈ 1. The angles are measured from the normal (the line perpendicular to the surface).

2. Snell’s law

n1 sin i = n2 sin r

For light going from air (n1 ≈ 1) into glass (n2 = n): n = sin i / sin r. Going into a denser medium, the ray bends towards the normal (r < i).

3. The straight-line graph

Plot sin i (y-axis) against sin r (x-axis). A straight line through the origin verifies Snell’s law, and

n = slope

4. Method (ray tracing)

Mark the outline of the block on paper; send a narrow ray into it; mark two dots on the incident ray and two on the emergent ray; remove the block, join the dots, draw the ray inside the block and the normal at the point of entry; measure i and r with a protractor.

Apparatus: rectangular glass block, ray box with a single slit (or a laser pointer), protractor, ruler, sharp pencil, white paper.
