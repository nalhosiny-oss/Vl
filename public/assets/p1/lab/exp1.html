Experiment 1: Vectors — The Force Table

Adding two forces graphically, analytically (components) and experimentally with an equilibrant

⚠️ Safety: hang the masses gently and keep feet away from under the hangers — a slipping string drops the masses. Hold the ring with the pin in place while you change masses.

Pre-lab questions

Answer before you come to the lab. The correction appears as soon as you choose.

Theory

A scalar has magnitude only; a vector has magnitude and direction. The sum of vectors is called the resultant R = F1 + F2. Linked to Chapter 1 — Vectors of the lectures.

1. Graphical (head-to-tail) method

Choose a scale (e.g. 1 cm = 20 g). Draw F1 from the origin, then draw F2 starting at the head of F1. The resultant runs from the origin to the head of the last vector. Measure its length (→ magnitude) and its angle with a protractor.

2. Analytical (component) method

Rx = F1 cos θ1 + F2 cos θ2 Ry = F1 sin θ1 + F2 sin θ2 R = √(Rx² + Ry²) θR = tan⁻¹(Ry / Rx)

Check the quadrant! A calculator gives tan⁻¹ between −90° and +90°. If Rx < 0 add 180°; if the answer is negative add 360° so that 0° ≤ θR < 360°. For two forces you can also use the law of cosines: R = √(F1² + F2² + 2F1F2 cos θ) where θ is the angle between them.

3. Experimental method — the equilibrant

The ring of the force table is in equilibrium when the vector sum of all forces on it is zero. The force that balances F1 and F2 is the equilibrant:

E = −R → E = R , θE = θR ± 180°

The forces are produced by hanging masses, so we measure them in grams-force (g): a mass of 100 g pulls with a force of 0.100 × 9.8 = 0.98 N. Because every force is multiplied by the same g, the vector sum can be done directly in grams.

Apparatus: force table with degree scale, 3 pulleys, ring and centre pin, strings, 3 mass hangers (50 g each), slotted masses, ruler, protractor, graph paper.

Simulation & measurement

Total mass on each string includes its 50 g hanger. Set F1 and F2, then adjust the third pulley (F3) until the ring is centred around the pin. Pulleys have a little friction, so a small range of settings will balance.
