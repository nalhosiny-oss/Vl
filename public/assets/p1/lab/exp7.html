Theory

Linked to Chapter 9 — Light and Optics (images formed by spherical mirrors) of the lectures.

1. Spherical mirrors

A spherical mirror is part of a sphere. In a concave (converging) mirror the inner surface reflects. Important points on the principal axis:

Pole P — centre of the mirror surfaceCentre of curvature C — centre of the spherePrincipal focus F — where reflected parallel rays meet

focal length f = PF radius of curvature R = PC = 2f

Concave mirrors are used in car headlights, satellite dishes, shaving mirrors and astronomical telescopes.

2. The mirror equation

When the object is farther than F, the reflected rays meet in front of the mirror and form a real, inverted image that can be caught on a screen. With the object distance u and image distance v measured from the pole (positive in front of the mirror):

1/f = 1/u + 1/v → f = u v / (u + v)

When the object is at C (u = 2f), the image is also at C, real, inverted and the same size.

3. The 1/u – 1/v graph

Plot 1/v (y-axis) against 1/u (x-axis) with the same scale on both axes. The line has slope −1 and cuts the axes at A and B, each equal to 1/f:

f = 2 / (A + B)
Apparatus: concave mirror in a holder, illuminated object (wire gauze with an arrow), small white screen beside the object, optical bench with metre scale.
