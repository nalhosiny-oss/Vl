Theory

Linked to Chapter 4 — Energy of a System (work done by a spring, Hooke’s law) of the lectures.

1. Hooke’s law

Within the elastic limit, the force needed to stretch a spring is proportional to its extension Δx. The spring pulls back with a restoring force of the same size in the opposite direction:

Fapplied = k Δx Fspring = −k Δx

k is the spring constant (stiffness) in N/m. The minus sign means the spring force is opposite to the extension.

2. The applied force

The load is the weight of the slotted masses: F = m g with m in kg and g = 9.8 m/s² (100 g → 0.98 N).

3. The straight-line graph

Plot the applied force F (N) on the y-axis against the extension Δx (m) on the x-axis. The points lie on a straight line and

k = slope = ΔF / Δ(Δx)

If you plot the extension on the y-axis instead, the slope is 1/k, not k.

4. Loading and unloading

Read the pointer while adding masses and again while removing them, and use the mean. If the unloading readings do not return to the loading readings, the spring has been stretched beyond its elastic limit.

Apparatus: spiral spring with pointer and scale pan (pan + pointer = 20 g, already hanging), rigid stand and clamp, vertical metre rule, six 50 g slotted masses.
