Experiment 2: The Simple Pendulum

Simple harmonic motion · measuring the free-fall acceleration g from a T² – L graph

⚠️ Safety: clamp the stand firmly to the bench so it cannot tip, keep the swinging bob away from faces, and keep the amplitude small.

Pre-lab questions

Answer before you come to the lab. The correction appears as soon as you choose.

Theory

A simple pendulum is a small heavy bob of mass M on a light string. The length L is measured from the point of suspension to the centre of the bob. Linked to Chapter 2 (free fall, g) and Chapter 3 (weight Mg, Newton’s second law) of the lectures.

1. Restoring force

When the bob is pulled aside by an angle θ, the component of its weight along the arc pulls it back:

F = −Mg sin θ ≈ −Mg θ = −(Mg / L) x (θ small, θ in radians, x = Lθ)

The force is proportional to the displacement and opposite to it, so the motion is simple harmonic. Newton’s second law gives a = −(g/L) x, and the periodic time is

T = 2π √(L / g)

Notice that T does not depend on the mass of the bob, and (for small angles) not on the amplitude.

2. The straight-line graph

Squaring both sides:

T² = (4π² / g) L → slope = 4π² / g → g = 4π² / slope

Plot T² (s²) on the y-axis against L (m) on the x-axis. The points lie on a straight line through the origin.

3. Good practice

• Keep the amplitude small (less than about 10°). • Time 10 complete oscillations (there and back = one oscillation) and divide by 10; this makes the reaction-time error 10 times smaller. • Start and stop the watch when the bob passes the lowest point (it moves fastest there, so the instant is sharp). • Reject a run if the bob swings in an ellipse.

Apparatus: pendulum bob (diameter 2.0 cm) on a cotton thread, clamp and stand with split cork, metre rule, stopwatch, fiducial mark (pointer) under the lowest point.
